Case Study 01 · Data Quality

When is raw data actually ready for analytics?

A production-inspired data quality case from my SNAP Analytics Platform: transforming public Excel reports into Trusted Silver through explicit data contracts, business validation, reconciliation, and a publication gate.

SNAP Analytics Platform · Python · Pandas · Parquet · Data Quality

The Context

I built this case using public Texas SNAP reporting data. Although the source arrived in Excel, it was designed primarily for human reporting rather than analytical pipelines.

Before building metrics or dashboards, I needed to answer a more fundamental question: when is transformed data actually trustworthy enough for downstream analytics?

I worked across two source domains: county-level SNAP caseload data and regional processing-timeliness reports. Together, they exposed production-style problems involving source structure, analytical grain, reference data, data types, validation rules, and pipeline failure behavior.

What Made the Source Risky?

Examples discovered during profiling and end-to-end testing

County-level files contained non-geographic reporting entities such as State Total, State Office, and Call Centers.

A validation reference initially contained only 9 counties, creating thousands of false validation failures.

A monetary field that appeared integer-like in most records contained real cents and required a different data type contract.

Timeliness workbooks contained two logical tables in one worksheet: Applications and Redeterminations, mixed with titles, blank rows, TOTAL rows, and notes.

Numeric-looking region identifiers such as 01 needed to remain strings because they represented categorical codes rather than measures.

Some source categories were undocumented, meaning they could not safely be guessed, remapped, or silently deleted.

The Trusted Silver Workflow

I separated transformation from trust. Successfully cleaning and converting a dataset did not automatically make it safe for downstream analytics.

Bronze → Structure Cleaning → Standardization → Type Conversion → Business Validation → Validation Gate → Trusted Silver
01

Profile the source

Understand structure, value patterns, missingness, categories, and source-specific anomalies before changing data.

02

Clean the structure

Separate analytical records from titles, notes, aggregate rows, and confirmed out-of-grain entities. For semi-structured Excel reports, logical sections are identified using business headers rather than fixed row positions.

03

Standardize representations

Correct source-specific representation issues without inventing new business meaning. Representation cleanup and business validation remain separate responsibilities.

04

Enforce type contracts

Choose target data types from business semantics rather than simply selecting whichever cast succeeds. Counts remain integer-compatible, identifiers remain strings, and monetary values preserve decimal precision when required.

05

Validate business rules

Check required fields, valid reporting entities, uniqueness at the analytical grain, nonnegative measures, cross-field constraints, and source reconciliation controls.

06

Gate publication

PASS publishes Trusted Silver. WARNING preserves evidence without necessarily blocking publication. FAIL generates validation evidence, blocks publication, and raises an error.

Two Failures That Changed the Design

The most useful lessons came from cases where the pipeline failed even though the first explanation turned out to be wrong.

01 · Validate the Validator

The Eligibility pipeline initially reported 2,981 invalid reporting entities.

Instead of assuming that most of the source data was bad, I profiled the failing population and inspected the validation reference.

The real problem was the validator: the known-county reference contained only 9 counties.

I replaced it with the full 254-county reference and treated reference data as a governed dependency rather than assuming the validation rule itself was always correct.

02 · Let Business Meaning Drive Data Types

A SNAP payment field failed an integer conversion because the raw source contained values with real cents.

Instead of truncating the value or switching types simply to make the pipeline pass, I reconciled the failed record against the raw Excel source.

The decimal value was legitimate business data.

The correct fix was therefore to update the target schema and preserve exact monetary precision.

How I Troubleshot Pipeline Failures

Rather than immediately changing code when a pipeline failed, I used a structured troubleshooting path to narrow the problem first.

Stage → Failed Rule → Affected Column → Row Pattern → Raw Source Evidence

This distinction mattered because a pipeline failure could come from transformation logic, source data, reference data, or an incorrect business assumption.

For example, when a Timeliness downstream input was missing, I traced the dependency upstream instead of changing Silver logic. The raw files existed, but the Bronze dataset had never been created because the ingestion process did not yet include Timeliness.

The fix belonged in the ingestion layer, not the downstream transformation.

Production Decisions

01

Analytical grain determines the data population

Eligibility was defined at County × Month grain. State Total, State Office, and Call Centers therefore did not belong in the county-level analytical population, even when they contained legitimate source values.

02

Source totals are controls, not analytical records

TOTAL rows were useful for reconciling extracted detail records, but publishing them alongside detail rows would create mixed grain and double counting.

03

Unknown categories should not be guessed

Undocumented source categories were preserved and flagged rather than silently mapped or deleted until their business meaning could be confirmed.

04

Warnings and failures have different meanings

Blocking integrity problems stop publication. Non-blocking anomalies remain visible as warnings so the pipeline preserves evidence without treating every unusual value as a production failure.

05

Source metrics are not automatically governed metrics

Timeliness source percentages are retained for reconciliation, while the governed analytical rate is calculated from underlying timely and disposed counts.

The Result

3,048
Trusted Eligibility records

254 Texas counties × 12 reporting months, published at a clear County × Month analytical grain.

2
Trusted Silver domains

Eligibility/Caseload and Timeliness are now validated upstream datasets ready for Gold dimensional modeling.

Timeliness is modeled separately at Region × Reporting Month × Processing Type, preserving Applications and Redeterminations as distinct operational processes.

With both Silver domains trusted, the project can now move into Gold dimensional modeling and governed metric design without rebuilding business logic directly from raw reports.

What I Took Away

The most important lesson was that data quality is not simply a cleaning step.

It requires explicit decisions about analytical grain, business meaning, schema, reference data, acceptable exceptions, validation severity, and failure behavior.

Automated tests verify the rules I wrote. Human validation helps confirm that I wrote the right rules.

Trusted Silver is earned through validation, not simply through transformation.
